# Padel training system: Neil

Handed over from an earlier agent session (Oct 3, 2026). Companion to [CONTEXT.md](CONTEXT.md).

## Who
~4.0 padel player, Moss Beach CA. Co-founder/CTO of Park Padel. Coach: Navarro. Plays 1-3x/week: league, casual, periodic private lessons. Records matches, then narrates a point-by-point video review; those narrated (auto-transcribed, often garbled) reviews are the primary input.

**Goal:** play through 3 full hard sets without physical or technical collapse, and raise overall level.

## Coaching method
1. Record match, review footage, narrate: what he tried, what happened, and whether the breakdown was technical, tactical or physical.
2. Transcribe (expect fragments).
3. Log each transcript into a skills tracker: session entry + week-over-week trend on the single current focus area.
4. **Uniform-attention rule:** he reviews the whole match evenly, so a previously flagged weakness going unmentioned is soft evidence it improved, not missing data.

## Current focus block (one at a time, by design)
**Contact point on lob returns / bandeja.** Everything else is logged but parked.

Failure chain: starts moving too late -> sets feet too early -> not behind the ball -> hits from underneath -> contact too high -> ball bounces big and gets attacked.

Checklist (his wording):
- Start moving the moment the opponent gears up for the lob, not when it's in the air
- Rotate and move back, 3 powerful steps, don't set feet early
- Get behind the ball before contact
- Give yourself space; don't hit from directly under the ball
- Contact at forehead/head height
- Goal: ball takes its **second** bounce at the wall, not the first; slow and controlled

## Root-cause thesis
Nearly every technical issue traces to **late initiation and tennis motor patterns** that return under time pressure. One habit with many faces:
- Closed racket face on bandeja setup (padel wants it open)
- Elbow rising, coming over the top on high forehands/backhands
- "Swooping" topspin loop on approach shots and short volleys
- Take-back way behind the head, follow-through over the shoulder
- Waiting for the ball to drop instead of taking it early

## Navarro's inputs
- **Unification:** short middle volley, bajada off the back wall, and bandeja are the same ball: come down on it and **push**. Vary speed, height, spin; push, don't swing. (Neil's nuance: "come down" without "push" swapped a bouncy-ball problem for a netted-ball problem.)
- **Wait for the right ball:** it's rarer than you think. At net vs. opponent at back, three cross-court balls: short chiquita (just make contact), simple volley (pressure, compact, into the feet), too-high forehand/backhand (handle, don't attack). For all: feet or middle, make it die before the glass.
- **Groundstrokes:** no wrist coming up on the ball; racket low; racket back *before the bounce*; push forward not up; no shoulder follow-through; compact.
- **Groundstrokes vs. net players:** feet / body / high to the backhand / full lob. Advancing to net is a read, not automatic.

## Mental game (active since late Sep 2026)
Key line: *"I get more upset when I make mistakes. They compound."* Errors came on **simple** balls, so it's an attention problem, not shot selection.

Framework (Gallwey, *Inner Game of Tennis*; Afremow, *The Champion's Mind*):
1. **Pre-match:** process goal, never outcome- or opponent-based.
2. **During the point:** give attention a sensory job (watch ball to contact), don't self-instruct.
3. **After an error:** fixed routine: turn away, adjust strings, breathe, face forward. No verdict.
4. **On review:** judgment belongs here. Self-criticism is his biggest asset in review and biggest liability mid-match.

## Physical context
- Footwork drill (12 min, 45s on / 15s off): calf pumps, boxer bounce, split-step figure 8, 2 forward 2 back, lateral bounds, back glass movement, low lateral steps, bandeja & volleys, lateral skier, fast feet, L-shape turns, figure of 8.
- "Strong engine, weak brakes": eccentric-heavy drilling needs load management. Right glute under-recruitment, left calf endurance deficit, vision-dependent balance.
- **Meal timing (GLP-1 slows gastric emptying):** a fat/protein meal under ~2 h before a match reliably causes nausea-type fatigue mid-match (cost a set led 5-3). Rule: real meal 3+ h before; only light fast carbs inside 1 h. Expects to come off the medication within weeks of goal weight.

## Data
- Whoop: per-session strain, zone distribution, avg HR, steps. Example: private lesson 23% Z5 / strain 17.1 vs. easy league match 0% Z5 / strain 14.1.
- Strava (running), Tonal (strength), **Withings (body composition)**.
- Cross-reference subjective session quality with objective intensity; watch for technique degrading in high-strain or late-session windows.
